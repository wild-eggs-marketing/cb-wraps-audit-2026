## Unused assets, ranked by value

**1. The 15 location pages — data fully bought, template throwing it away.** All 15 `Locations` records are 100% populated: meta title, meta description, summary, 3 FAQs, amenities, service options, payments, hours, hours-note, lat/lng, two order URLs. The pages are live at `/locations/{slug}` and render most of the body content. But: every one serves the generic site `<title>` ("Crazy Bowls & Wraps | Fast, Fresh, Ready") instead of the CMS `Meta Title`; the meta description ships an unresolved literal `{Location Name}` token; and there is **zero JSON-LD on any location page** — no LocalBusiness, no Restaurant, no FAQPage, despite `fixes/schema/locations.json` having been written in June 2026 and never shipped. Fifteen identical titles across the site's second-biggest traffic cluster. Worth: `/locations` already pulls 19,663 sessions/mo at the worst engagement rate of any top page (46.5%). *Estimate:* 15 pages × 150–400 sessions/mo of city/neighborhood/"near me" intent = 2,000–6,000 sessions/mo net-new. Effort: ~1 day of Framer template binding plus a CMS-driven schema block. Highest value-to-effort item in this whole review.

**2. Catering — a live revenue line with 27 empty pages.** 1,105 sessions/mo, 59% engagement. The `Menu` CMS holds 27 catering SKUs with real prices ($1.99 bottled water to $140 Fajita Bar; wrap platter $54, box lunch $13.20/head). All 27 are in the sitemap — and all 27 render as empty shells (`/menu/catering-fajita-bar` returns 25KB with no `<h1>`). Only 2 of 27 carry calories; 3 carry allergens; 1 carries a dietary tag. *Estimate:* at a $250–400 average catering order, 1,105 sessions converting at 1% ≈ $3.3k/mo. Getting to 2.5% ≈ **+$60k/yr**. Corporate catering buyers are exactly the people who need "can you feed a gluten-free and a vegan attendee" answered before they call — and you already own the data to answer it. Effort: fill 27 CMS records, render the template, add per-location catering blocks.

**3. Tacos are a missing category, not a missing item.** The FDA export has 9 Tacos — BBQ, Buffalo, Caesar, Jerk, Mediterranean, Pesto, Power, Teriyaki, Thai — the identical flavour lineup that already gets 14 individual `/menu/{flavour}-bowl` pages and 8 `/menu/{flavour}-wrap` pages. Tacos get one merged `/menu/tacos`. Catering already sells a Mini Taco Platter ($54), so the kitchen makes them. "Tacos" search volume dwarfs "bowls" in any US metro. Nine pages, data already in hand. Also unlisted: 2 Broth Bowls (soups — a whole winter daypart), Healthy Burrito, Fruit & Feta and Grilled Veggie salads.

**4. The nutrition→local bridge nobody has spotted.** Eight modifier rows are location-gated: *Gluten Free Tortilla* and *Plant Based Chicken* are **only at Forsyth and O'Fallon**. That means two location pages can honestly claim "gluten-free wraps available here" and "plant-based chicken here" — the single strongest local differentiator in the dataset, and it's currently buried in a calorie-delta table. Effort: near zero.

**5. The Blog is built and hidden.** 14 published posts (2,100–3,300 words of HTML each), 4 categories, live at `/blog`. Not one appears in the June crawl's link graph, and location pages link to blog posts but nothing links back. Two are press pieces establishing the PG Growth / Wild Eggs story, and `stlmag-plant-based-cbw` documents a real collaboration with STL Veg Girl Caryn Dugan — a named local influencer with an existing audience. That's the only genuine link-earning asset here: "30 years of kale in St. Louis, since 1997" plus a nutrition calculator no regional competitor has. Pitch to STL food press, not to a link farm.

**6. Two orphaned pages printing money.** `/choose-your-location` — 1,060 sessions, **92% engagement**, 44 words, zero inbound internal links. `/fall-menu` — 595 sessions, orphaned. Seasonal LTO demand exists and nothing captures it; there are already 4 `lto-menu-items` records. Effort: hours.

**7. Zero review content.** 15 locations, no `aggregateRating`, no Review schema, no reviews on any page — despite `ACTION-PLAN.md` Task scoping it. Cheapest trust signal available.

## Nothing here

- **Drinks (25 SKUs), sauces (15 per container), kids sides, desserts.** Nobody searches "Crazy Bowls kombucha calories." These belong inside the calculator as modifiers, which is where they are. The one exception worth 20 minutes: Diet Coke and Vitamin Water at 0 cal, and four kombuchas at 20–30 cal, are useful *content* inside a "what to drink" answer — not pages.
- **`/allergen-menu` as a standalone page.** 99 sessions. The 94% engagement rate is real but the volume is a rounding error; fold it into the calculator and 301 it, as already planned.
- **`/Hours/*` and `/louisville_starters/*` (13 old WordPress orphans).** Dead artefacts of the old CMS, 17–35 words each. Redirect and forget.
- **`Blog Category` expansion.** Four categories over 14 posts is already thin. Don't build more taxonomy; build more posts.

## What I'd sell next

**A multi-location local SEO programme, priced as a retainer, sold on the expansion story — not on the SEO.**

The argument writes itself from their own blog: CBW joined PG Growth in March 2024, sits alongside Wild Eggs under shared ownership, has converted a Wild Eggs kitchen in Jeffersontown into a CBW virtual kitchen, and per Andy Abbajay plans "a few stand-alone Crazy Bowls locations in the next 12–16 months," with franchising rights across eight states. Sixteen stores today. The `Locations` collection already has a `Coming Soon` boolean that is `false` on all 15 records — someone built for expansion and nobody has used it.

So the sell is not "fix your title tags." It is: *every new store you open is worth roughly 200–400 organic sessions a month, and right now your template captures none of it. We build the location engine once — CMS-bound titles, LocalBusiness + FAQPage schema, per-location catering and nutrition modules, GBP alignment — and every subsequent opening inherits it for free.* That converts a one-off nutrition project into per-store recurring work and makes the agency the default vendor for the franchise rollout playbook. Anchor it at the catering number (+$60k/yr, defensible from the CMS prices) so the retainer pays for itself before the local traffic even lands.
