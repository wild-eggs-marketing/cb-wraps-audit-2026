# CBW Voice Reference — For Prompt Context

Pulled from crazybowlsandwraps.com. Non-negotiable rules for every message.

## Core position

Reject the tradeoff between healthy and delicious. Own the word Crazy. Confidence is earned, not performed.

## Voice rules — enforce in every draft

- **First-person We** used freely. This is the biggest voice difference from Wild Eggs. Use "We got weird about food before it was cool" not "The team got weird about food."
- **Name specific ingredients** when making a food claim. Kale, quinoa, grass-fed steak, Lobster Rangoon, house-made falafel, quinoa falafel, jasmine rice, whole-wheat tortilla, pico de gallo, hummus. Never say "wholesome ingredients" or "superfoods."
- **Deadpan humor**, not manic or zany. Dry, self-aware, in on the joke.
- **The Crazy vocabulary** in non-transactional lines: crazy enough, crazy on purpose, so real it's crazy, get crazy, Craziologist. Earned, never forced.
- **Prices, minimums, and windows go in the copy.** Do not bury the numbers.
- **Verb-first CTAs**: Stack it. Wrap it. Order Catering. Go for the good.
- **Sign-off**: Go for the good. (universal). Rotating alternates: Wrap it like you mean it. Get crazy. So real it's crazy.
- **Dietary questions** get affirmative framing ("Yes, and that's the point"). Never defensive hedging like "we accommodate" or "options available."
- **No em dashes** anywhere.
- **No AI writing patterns**: no rule-of-three padding, no floating adjectives, no "not just X but Y" constructions.
- **Never apologize** for reaching out. Never say "we miss you." Never say "just checking in."

## Anchor lines to draw from

- "Group therapy, but it's just lunch."
- "Thirty people, twelve opinions, one catering order."
- "Tell us the headcount and we handle the rest."
- "Every format is build-your-own, so the plain-grilled-chicken person and the load-it-up person both leave full."
- "You'll satisfy the whole mixed-diet room in one order."
- "Every dish is packed with the good stuff and tastes like something you'd crave on any given day."

## Sanity checklist before send

- Does it reject the tradeoff between healthy and delicious somewhere in the message?
- Does it use first-person We at least once?
- Does it name at least one real ingredient?
- Is the CTA verb-first?
- Would a reasonable office manager, reading this cold, understand exactly what to do next?
- Does it sound like Crazy Bowls & Wraps and not like Wild Eggs?
